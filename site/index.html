Notary basics

Never used a notary before? Start here.

Most people need a notary only a few times in their lives, so it's normal not to know how this works. Here's the short version.

What a notary does

A notary public is an impartial witness commissioned by the state. I check who you are, make sure you're signing by your own choice, and complete the notarial certificate with my signature and official seal.

I don't check whether your document is accurate or legally sound. That part stays with you and whoever prepared it.

What "mobile" means

A mobile notary performs the same notarization you'd get at an office, at a place you choose. That's useful when the signer can't easily travel, when several people need to sign together, or when your schedule doesn't fit office hours.

Mobile, office or remote online: which fits?

TypeHow it worksGood when
Mobile notary The notary travels to you and notarizes in person. You pay a travel fee on top of the notary fee. The signer is at home, in a hospital or in a care facility, or you'd simply like someone to come to you.
Office notary You bring your documents and ID to the notary's location during their hours. You have one simple document and can easily get there.
Remote online notary You meet by live video with a notary who holds Utah's separate remote notary certification. Your identity is checked electronically. You can't meet in person, and whoever is receiving the document accepts remote notarization.

My appointments are in person. I don't offer remote online notarization.

The five kinds of notarization in Utah

Acknowledgment
You tell me, in person, that you signed the document willingly for its stated purpose.
Jurat
You sign in front of me and swear or affirm that the statements in the document are true.
Signature witnessing
You show ID and sign the document while I watch.
Copy certification
I certify that a photocopy matches the original. Utah allows this only when the original is neither a public record nor a publicly recorded document.
Oath or affirmation
You make a spoken vow in front of me, under penalty of perjury.

Your document usually shows which one it needs in the notary wording near the signature line. If it doesn't, ask whoever requested the document. I can describe each type, but I can't choose for you.

Documents people often bring

Need an official copy of a birth, death or marriage certificate? Those come from the office that issued the record, not from a notary.

How to choose a notary

  • They hold a current notary commission in the state where you're signing.
  • They ask every signer for valid photo ID.
  • They tell you the full cost, travel included, before the appointment.
  • They're clear that they can't give legal advice.
  • They're easy to reach and confirm your appointment in writing.

A notary is not a lawyer

I am not an attorney licensed to practice law in Utah and may not give legal advice about immigration or any other legal matter or accept fees for legal advice.

In practice, that means I can't explain what your document means, help you fill it in, or tell you which kind of notarization you need. For those questions, talk to an attorney or to the agency that asked for the document.

Notary rules on this page follow the Utah Notaries Public Reform Act (Utah Code Title 46, Chapter 1).

Before your appointment

What to bring

  • Valid government photo ID for every signer It has to be unexpired and show your photo, signature and physical description. A driver's license, state ID card or passport works. A Utah driving privilege card is not accepted for notarization.
  • Your documents, complete but unsigned Fill in every blank, then wait to sign until I'm with you. Bring every page, not only the signature page.
  • Any witnesses your document requires Some documents need one or two witnesses in addition to the notary. Check yours, and have them there with their own photo ID.
  • Everyone who is signing Each person whose signature I'm notarizing has to be there in person.

Frequently asked questions

How do I book, and how will I know it's confirmed?

Choose a time on the calendar on this page, say whether you'd like to meet in Fort Union or have me come to you, and tell me what you need notarized. You'll get a confirmation by email with the date and time. If I have a question about your documents, I'll contact you before the appointment.

How much does it cost?

$10 per signature. If we meet in the Fort Union area, there's no travel fee. If I come to you, travel is $0.70 per mile, round trip from Midvale, and we agree on it before the appointment. New clients get 10% off their first appointment. The full list is under Pricing.

Where in Fort Union do we meet?

At a public place in the Fort Union area of Midvale. I'll send you the exact spot when I confirm your appointment.

When do I pay?

In person, once the notarization is done. Nothing is charged when you book online.

Should I sign my document before you arrive?

No. Wait until I'm with you. Some notarizations require me to watch you sign, and waiting keeps things simple for all of them.

What ID do you accept?

Unexpired identification issued by a government that includes your photo, signature and physical description, such as a driver's license or state ID card, or an unexpired passport from any country. Utah law doesn't allow a driving privilege card to be used for notarization.

What if the signer doesn't have a valid ID?

Utah law allows a signer to be identified by a credible witness in some situations. Contact me before you book so we can talk through whether that applies to you.

Can you come to a hospital or care facility?

Yes. The signer needs acceptable photo ID and has to be alert, able to communicate with me directly, and signing by their own choice. If any of that is in doubt on the day, I can't complete the notarization, and we can try again another time.

Do you speak Spanish?

Yes, fluently. I can hold your whole appointment in Spanish.

Can you tell me which notarization I need, or explain my document?

No. I'm not an attorney, so I can't give legal advice, explain what a document means or choose the type of notarization. The agency or person who asked for the document can tell you what they need.

Do you help with immigration forms or paperwork?

No. I don't fill out immigration forms or give immigration advice. For that, talk to an immigration attorney or an accredited representative.

Can you notarize a copy of my birth certificate?

No. For an official copy of a birth, death or marriage certificate, contact the office that issued it.

Do you keep a record of the notarization?

Yes. I record each notarization in my notary journal, including your name, your signature and the ID you showed me.

What if I need to cancel or change my time?

Your confirmation email has links to cancel or reschedule. You can also call or text me.
